City officials announced Tuesday that the long-delayed renovation of the central library will finally begin next month, more than three years after voters approved the bond measure that funds it. The project, originally budgeted at 24 million dollars, has grown to an estimated 31 million dollars due to rising construction costs and an expanded scope that now includes a dedicated children's wing and an upgraded HVAC system throughout the building.

"We know residents have been frustrated by how long this has taken," said the city's director of public works at a press conference outside the library's main entrance. "Between supply chain issues, a redesign to meet updated accessibility standards, and the usual permitting delays, we ended up well past our original timeline. But the plan we have now is genuinely better than what we started with."

The library will remain open during the first phase of construction, which focuses on the building's exterior and structural reinforcement, before closing for approximately eight months starting in the spring for interior work. A temporary branch will operate out of a storefront two blocks away during the closure, offering a reduced but functional collection along with computer access and the library's popular after-school programs.

Community reaction has been mixed. Several residents who attended the announcement welcomed the news, while others questioned why the city had not communicated the revised budget earlier. The city council is expected to formally approve the final construction contract at its meeting next week.
